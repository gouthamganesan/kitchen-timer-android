# Design principles

Kitchen Timer is hardware skeuomorphism built from editable geometry. Its appeal depends on consistent material, hierarchy and response.

- **One visual centre.** The disappearing disc carries the screen; the LCD supplies precision. Avoid redundant titles, status labels and counters.
- **Light has an origin.** Ambient highlights agree on an upper-left source. Dark mode lights the LCD and face locally, leaving casing and buttons recognizable.
- **Depth explains action.** A button has a socket, raised cap and travel. The rocker moves inside its own housing; no rectangular Material ripple surrounds its icons.
- **Feedback follows intent.** Detents happen during winding. Passive countdown, restoration and theme changes do not imitate physical interaction.
- **Subtraction changes spacing.** Removing a control requires recomposing the remaining space. Preserve usable targets and stable geometry across duration formats and font sizes.
- **Focus stays simple.** The original ivory/red and charcoal/amber faces remain. Centre tap starts/pauses; presets are absent.
